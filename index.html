Research Use Cases

Five questions you’ll ask every week

These are the research jobs MonsterGPT is built for. Every panel below is a clearly labelled illustrative interface example — it shows layout and capability, not live NFL statistics.

01 · Matchup research

Frame the game before you look at a single number

Ask how two teams stack up stylistically — tempo, personnel groupings, how each side tends to attack and defend — and get a structured breakdown you can actually act on as research.

“How do these two offences want to play, and where do their tendencies collide?”

02 · Line-movement research

See how the number travelled, not just where it landed

Ask MonsterGPT to walk you through the history of a number — when it moved, in which direction and what was happening around each step — so you read the whole week instead of a single snapshot.

“Show me how this number has moved since it opened and what lined up with each step.”

03 · Injury & availability

Understand which absences actually change the game

Not every name on a report matters equally. Ask what a listed status would realistically do to a game plan, snap distribution or role — and get the reasoning, not a headline.

“If this role is limited, what changes downstream?”

04 · Player-prop research

Ask what the shape of a role really looks like

Move past a single average. Ask about usage patterns, game-script dependency and the situations that drive a role’s volume, so you can judge a market on more than one number.

“What drives this role’s volume, and when does it disappear?”

05 · Situational trends

Test a trend before you trust it

Ask MonsterGPT to pressure-test the pattern you think you’re seeing: how big the sample is, what conditions it depends on, and whether it survives when you change the framing.

“Is this trend real, or is it a small sample wearing a costume?”
